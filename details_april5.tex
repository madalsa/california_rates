\section{Baseline Allowance Overview}
% =============================================================================

California's baseline allowance program provides a per-kWh credit on electricity consumption up to a threshold, protecting low-usage customers from high volumetric rates. The threshold varies by geography (PUMA/climate zone) and season, reflecting differences in climate-driven energy needs.

% =============================================================================
\section{Baseline Allowance Structure}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Allowance Determination}

Each building $i$ is assigned a daily baseline allowance based on its PUMA (Public Use Microdata Area), which serves as a proxy for climate zone:

\begin{align}
    B_i^{\text{daily, summer}} &= \text{baseline\_puma}(i, \text{summer}) \quad \text{(kWh/day)} \\
    B_i^{\text{daily, winter}} &= \text{baseline\_puma}(i, \text{winter}) \quad \text{(kWh/day)}
\end{align}

These values are sourced from the \texttt{baseline\_puma} sheet in each utility's rate data file (\texttt{retail\_rates\_data\_PGE.xlsx}, \texttt{retail\_rates\_data\_SCE.xlsx}, \texttt{retail\_rates\_data\_SDGE.xlsx}).

\subsection{Monthly Baseline Threshold}

The monthly baseline allowance for building $i$ in month $m$ is:

\begin{equation}
    B_i^m = \begin{cases}
        B_i^{\text{daily, summer}} \times D_m & \text{if } m \in \{6, 7, 8, 9, 10\} \text{ (Jun--Oct)} \\
        B_i^{\text{daily, winter}} \times D_m & \text{if } m \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 11, 12\} \text{ (Nov--May)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

where $D_m$ is the number of days in month $m$.

% =============================================================================
\section{Baseline Credit Calculation}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Credit Rate}

Each utility applies a constant baseline credit rate $b$ (\$/kWh) to consumption within the baseline threshold:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l c}
\toprule
\textbf{Utility} & \textbf{Baseline Credit Rate} $b$ \textbf{(\$/kWh)} \\
\midrule
PG\&E (E-TOU-C) & 0.09566 \\
SCE (TOU-D-4-9) & 0.10000 \\
SDG\&E (TOU-DR) & 0.11017 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Annual Baseline Credit}

The annual baseline credit for building $i$ is computed month by month:

\begin{equation}
    BL(i) = \sum_{m=1}^{12} b \cdot \min\!\left(Q_i^m,\; B_i^m\right)
\end{equation}

where $Q_i^m$ is the total electricity consumption (kWh) of building $i$ in month $m$.

\begin{itemize}
    \item If $Q_i^m \leq B_i^m$: the full monthly consumption receives the credit.
    \item If $Q_i^m > B_i^m$: only the baseline portion receives the credit; consumption above the threshold pays the full TOU rate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Effect on Bill}

The baseline credit is subtracted from gross volumetric charges before applying the CARE discount:

\begin{equation}
    \text{Bill}_i = \left[\underbrace{\sum_p Q_i^p \cdot r^p}_{\text{gross volumetric}} - \underbrace{BL(i)}_{\text{baseline credit}}\right] \times (1 - d_i) + \text{FC}_i
\end{equation}

where $r^p$ is the TOU rate for period $p$, $d_i$ is the CARE discount (0.35 for PG\&E, 0.325 for SCE, 0.37 for SDG\&E; 0 for non-CARE), and $\text{FC}_i$ is the annual fixed charge.

% =============================================================================
\section{Application Across Rate Scenarios}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Actual Tariff Rates}

For the status quo tariff (E-TOU-C, TOU-D-4-9, TOU-DR), the baseline credit is applied to the building's total metered consumption $Q_i^m$.

\subsection{Designed Rate Scenarios}

For designed rate scenarios (F0\_WF0\_ROE0, F50\_WF0\_ROE0, etc.), the same baseline credit rate $b$ and allowance thresholds $B_i^m$ are used. The credit is constant across all designed scenarios---it is a structural feature of the rate, not a policy lever.

The volumetric rates $\tilde{r}^p = s \cdot r^p$ change with the scenario scaling factor $s$, but the credit $b$ does not.

\subsection{Post-Adoption Scenarios (PV + Storage)}

For technology adopters with PV and battery storage, the baseline credit is applied to \textbf{grid import} rather than total consumption. After the LP-optimized battery dispatch determines the hourly grid import profile $g_t$, the monthly grid import is:

\begin{equation}
    G_i^m = \sum_{t \in m} g_t
\end{equation}

The baseline credit then becomes:

\begin{equation}
    BL_{\text{post}}(i) = \sum_{m=1}^{12} b \cdot \min\!\left(G_i^m,\; B_i^m\right)
\end{equation}

This reflects the fact that under net billing, the baseline credit applies to electricity purchased from the grid, not to self-consumed solar generation. A household with PV + storage that imports less from the grid receives a smaller baseline credit.

\section{Battery Dispatch Optimization}

For households with PV and battery storage, we solve a linear program (LP) to determine the optimal hourly battery charge/discharge schedule that minimizes the household's annual electricity cost. The LP is solved using the HiGHS solver via \texttt{scipy.optimize.linprog}.

\subsection{PV System Sizing}

PV capacity is determined before the LP, based on native (unscaled) annual demand:

\begin{equation}
    C_i = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{0.90 \times D_i^{\text{annual}}}{v^{\text{annual}}},\; C_{\min},\; C_{\max}\right)
\end{equation}

where $D_i^{\text{annual}}$ is the building's native annual electricity consumption (kWh), $v^{\text{annual}}$ is the annual generation per kW from the pvlib solar profile for the building's CEC climate zone, $C_{\min} = 3$ kW, and $C_{\max} = 15$ kW. The 90\% offset target reflects median residential PV sizing in California IOU territories.

The hourly solar generation profile is:
\begin{equation}
    V_t = C_i \cdot v_t \quad \forall \, t \in \{1, \ldots, 8760\}
\end{equation}
where $v_t$ is the per-kW hourly generation from pvlib (climate-zone specific).

\subsection{Battery Parameters}

The battery is modeled as a Tesla Powerwall equivalent with fixed parameters (not optimized):

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l c l}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
$E$ & 13.5 kWh & Energy capacity \\
$P_{\max}$ & 5.0 kW & Maximum charge/discharge power \\
$\eta_{\text{rt}}$ & 0.90 & Round-trip efficiency \\
$\eta = \sqrt{\eta_{\text{rt}}}$ & 0.949 & One-way efficiency \\
$s_0$ & $0.5 \times E = 6.75$ kWh & Initial state of charge \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{LP Formulation}

\subsubsection{Decision Variables}

Five variables per hour, $T = 8760$ hours, giving $5T = 43{,}800$ variables total:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l c l}
\toprule
\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Bounds} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
$g_t$ & $[0, \infty)$ & Grid import at hour $t$ (kWh) \\
$e_t$ & $[0,\; V_t + P_{\max}]$ & Grid export at hour $t$ (kWh) \\
$c_t$ & $[0,\; P_{\max}]$ & Battery charge at hour $t$ (kWh) \\
$d_t$ & $[0,\; P_{\max}]$ & Battery discharge at hour $t$ (kWh) \\
$s_t$ & $[0,\; E]$ & State of charge at hour $t$ (kWh) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The export upper bound $V_t + P_{\max}$ prevents the model from importing electricity from the grid solely to re-export it for profit. At any hour, exports cannot exceed the combined PV generation and maximum battery discharge.

\subsubsection{Objective Function}

Minimize the net annual electricity cost:

\begin{equation}
    \min \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( p_t \cdot g_t - q_t \cdot e_t \right)
\end{equation}

where $p_t$ is the hourly retail import rate (\$/kWh) from the TOU tariff, and $q_t$ is the hourly export compensation rate (\$/kWh) from the EEC schedule.

\subsubsection{Constraints}

\textbf{Energy balance} ($T$ equality constraints):
\begin{equation}
    g_t - e_t - c_t + d_t \cdot \eta = D_t - V_t \quad \forall \, t \in \{1, \ldots, T\}
\end{equation}

The left-hand side represents the net grid interaction plus battery flows. The right-hand side is the net load (demand minus solar generation). When $D_t - V_t > 0$, the household must import from the grid or discharge the battery. When $D_t - V_t < 0$, excess solar can charge the battery or be exported.

\textbf{SOC dynamics} ($T - 1$ equality constraints):
\begin{equation}
    s_t - s_{t-1} - c_t \cdot \eta + d_t = 0 \quad \forall \, t \in \{2, \ldots, T\}
\end{equation}

The state of charge increases by $c_t \cdot \eta$ when charging (accounting for one-way efficiency loss) and decreases by $d_t$ when discharging.

\textbf{Initial SOC}:
\begin{equation}
    s_1 - c_1 \cdot \eta + d_1 = 0.5 \times E
\end{equation}

The battery starts at 50\% capacity. This is a standard assumption; the choice has negligible effect on annual results since the battery cycles daily.

\subsection{Dispatch Reuse Across Rate Scenarios}

The LP is solved \textbf{once per building per adoption scenario} using the actual tariff rate array (E-TOU-C for PG\&E, TOU-D-4-9 for SCE, TOU-DR for SDG\&E). The resulting dispatch profile --- the hourly vectors $g_t$, $e_t$, $c_t$, $d_t$, $s_t$ --- is then reused to compute bills under all 8 rate scenarios (6 designed + 2 actual tariff variants).

This is valid because all rate scenarios share the same TOU period structure:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PG\&E}: Peak 4--9 PM, Off-peak all other hours (4 periods)
    \item \textbf{SCE}: Peak 4--9 PM, Mid-peak 9 PM--8 AM (winter only), Off-peak remainder (5 periods)
    \item \textbf{SDG\&E}: Peak 4--9 PM, Mid-peak 6 AM--4 PM \& 9--10 PM, Off-peak 10 PM--6 AM (6 periods)
\end{itemize}

The designed rate scenarios apply a uniform scaling factor $s$ to all TOU period rates (see Section~4 of \texttt{ratedesignaprl1.tex}). This preserves the \textit{timing} of peak and off-peak hours --- only the rate levels change. Since the optimal battery dispatch depends on \textit{when} rates are high vs.\ low (not the absolute level), the dispatch profile is invariant to uniform rate scaling.

\textbf{Result}: Each building requires only 2 LP solves total:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 1 LP for S2 (PV + storage): demand $D_t$ = native building load
    \item 1 LP for S3 (PV + storage + EV): demand $D_t$ = native load + EV charging
\end{itemize}

Each LP solve takes approximately 0.5 seconds (HiGHS solver). Bills for all 8 rate scenarios are computed from the shared dispatch using simple dot products ($\sim$0.001 seconds each).

\subsection{Bill Computation from Dispatch}

Given the dispatch profile $(g_t, e_t)$ from the LP, the bill under rate scenario $k$ is:

\begin{equation}
    \text{Bill}_i^{(k)} = \left[ \sum_t g_t \cdot r_t^{(k)} - BL_{\text{post}}(i) \right] \times (1 - d_i) - \sum_t e_t \cdot q_t + \text{FC}_i^{(k)}
\end{equation}

where $r_t^{(k)}$ is the hourly rate under scenario $k$, $BL_{\text{post}}(i)$ is the baseline credit applied to monthly grid imports (Section~3.4), $d_i$ is the CARE discount, $q_t$ is the EEC export rate, and $\text{FC}_i^{(k)}$ is the annual fixed charge under scenario $k$. The bill is floored at zero.

\section{EV Charging Profile}

\subsection{Daily Driving Distance}

Each EV-adopted building is assigned a persistent daily driving distance sampled from the empirical BEV daily vehicle miles traveled (DVMT) distribution (source: vehicletrends.us, BEV All). The inverse-CDF sampling ensures the distribution of driving distances across the building sample matches observed BEV usage patterns.

The mean daily VMT from this distribution is approximately 24 miles/day.

\subsection{Charging Parameters}

EV charging is modeled as Level 2 home charging with the following parameters:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l c l}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
Charger power & 7.2 kW & Level 2 (240V $\times$ 30A) \\
Efficiency & 3.0 mi/kWh & Wall-to-wheels \\
Start time & 10:00 PM & Daily charging start \\
Duration & Variable & Depends on daily miles driven \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Charging Profile Construction}

The daily energy requirement and charging duration for building $i$ are:

\begin{align}
    E_i^{\text{EV}} &= \frac{\text{daily\_miles}_i}{\text{efficiency}} = \frac{\text{daily\_miles}_i}{3.0} \quad \text{(kWh/day)} \\
    \tau_i &= \frac{E_i^{\text{EV}}}{P_{\text{charger}}} = \frac{E_i^{\text{EV}}}{7.2} \quad \text{(hours)}
\end{align}

The hourly charging profile is constructed as:

\begin{equation}
    \text{EV}_t = \begin{cases}
        P_{\text{charger}} = 7.2 \text{ kW} & \text{for full hours after 10 PM} \\
        P_{\text{charger}} \times (\tau_i - \lfloor \tau_i \rfloor) & \text{for the fractional last hour} \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The profile is identical every day of the year (no weekday/weekend or seasonal variation).

\textbf{Examples}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 24 mi/day $\rightarrow$ 8.0 kWh $\rightarrow$ 1.11 hours: 7.2 kW from 10:00--11:00 PM, 0.8 kW from 11:00--11:07 PM
    \item 40 mi/day $\rightarrow$ 13.3 kWh $\rightarrow$ 1.85 hours: 7.2 kW from 10:00--11:00 PM, 6.1 kW from 11:00--11:51 PM
    \item 60 mi/day $\rightarrow$ 20.0 kWh $\rightarrow$ 2.78 hours: 7.2 kW from 10:00 PM--12:00 AM, 5.6 kW from 12:00--12:47 AM
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Integration with Adoption Scenarios}

The EV charging profile enters the analysis in two adoption scenarios:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{S1 (EV only)}: The demand profile is $D_t + \text{EV}_t$. No PV or battery. Bills are computed directly from the augmented load profile (no LP).

    \item \textbf{S3 (PV + Storage + EV)}: The demand profile entering the LP is $D_t + \text{EV}_t$. The LP optimizes battery dispatch against the combined load minus solar:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{net\_load}_t = (D_t + \text{EV}_t) - V_t
    \end{equation}
    The battery can shift EV charging costs by discharging stored solar during the charging window, or by storing cheap off-peak grid electricity to offset the EV load.
\end{itemize}

Note that the EV charging schedule is \textit{not} optimized by the LP --- it is a fixed exogenous load profile. The LP optimizes only the battery dispatch given the total demand (building + EV) and solar generation.

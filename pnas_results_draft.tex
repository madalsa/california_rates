\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Revenue requirement structure and policy levers.}

California's three IOUs collect a combined \$42.1 billion in authorized revenue, of which residential customers fund \$17.5 billion (Fig.~1). The residential share varies: PG\&E residential customers pay 41\% of total revenue (\$8.2 billion), SCE pays 44\% (\$7.8 billion), and SDG\&E pays 37\% (\$1.6 billion). Transmission and distribution costs dominate the revenue requirement at 56--57\% of residential revenue across all three utilities. Generation and procurement account for 26--29\%.

The three policy levers we examine differ sharply in magnitude (Fig.~1, bottom right). T\&D costs represent the largest share of the revenue requirement at \$12.5 billion (PG\&E), \$10.1 billion (SCE), and \$2.4 billion (SDG\&E), defining the maximum scope for fixed charge restructuring. Wildfire liabilities are the second largest lever but vary dramatically by utility: PG\&E carries \$5.4 billion (27\% of residential revenue), reflecting its exposure in Northern California's fire-prone regions. SCE's wildfire burden is \$2.9 billion (17\%), and SDG\&E's is \$0.4 billion (10\%). The authorized return on equity generates \$2.2 billion annually for both PG\&E (at 10.28\% ROE) and SCE (at 10.33\%), and \$0.7 billion for SDG\&E (at 10.22\%). These three cost components are not mutually exclusive: wildfire costs are largely embedded in T\&D infrastructure, and the ROE is earned predominantly on T\&D assets (88--96\% of rate base).

\subsection*{Average bills across customer archetypes and rate scenarios.}

We compute annual bills for four customer archetypes under six rate scenarios across all three utilities (Fig.~2). Non-adopters pay \$2,900--\$3,300/yr under the reference TOU rate, with SDG\&E customers paying the most (\$3,283/yr) and SCE the least (\$2,886/yr). EV-only households pay approximately \$1,200--\$1,300 more than non-adopters (\$4,100--\$4,600/yr), reflecting the additional 2,900 kWh/yr from Level 2 home charging. PV-plus-storage households achieve near-zero bills (\$161--\$384/yr), a 90--95\% reduction from non-adopter levels. Households with all three technologies (PV, EV, and storage) pay \$591--\$1,065/yr, less than non-adopters but more than PV-only because EV charging adds load that solar and battery cannot fully offset.

Two patterns emerge across scenarios. First, the revenue-neutral scenarios (CPUC Fixed Charge and 50\% Fixed Charge) redistribute bills without changing the total: non-adopter bills shift modestly, while PV-plus-storage bills increase substantially because the fixed charge adds a cost floor that these households cannot avoid through self-generation. Second, wildfire removal produces the largest absolute bill reductions across all customer types, with PG\&E showing the steepest declines.

\subsection*{Bill changes differ by CARE status and technology adoption.}

We disaggregate bill changes from the reference rate by CARE status (Fig.~3). For non-adopters without distributed energy resources, the revenue-neutral CPUC fixed charge reduces bills for both CARE and non-CARE customers across PG\&E and SCE. The 50\% fixed charge produces a sharper split: CARE non-adopters save \$212/yr (PG\&E) and \$227/yr (SCE), while non-CARE non-adopters see small increases of \$50 (PG\&E) and \$25 (SDG\&E). SCE non-CARE customers see a slight decrease of \$24/yr. The direction of the non-CARE impact depends on the utility-specific consumption distribution and the CARE enrollment share.

Wildfire removal delivers the largest savings for all groups. PG\&E CARE non-adopters save \$451/yr and non-CARE save \$959/yr. The combined scenario (50\% fixed charges, wildfire removal, and 1\% ROE reduction) saves CARE customers \$418--\$554/yr (27--34\%) and non-CARE customers \$603--\$977/yr (11--27\%) depending on the utility.

For PV-plus-storage adopters, the pattern reverses. Non-CARE PV households see bill \textit{increases} of \$600--\$800/yr under 50\% fixed charges because the monthly fee (\$88--\$92/month) exceeds their near-zero volumetric charges. CARE PV households are partially shielded by the lower CARE fixed charge (\$6/month). Under wildfire removal, PV households see only modest reductions because their bills are already near zero under the reference rate.

EV-only households see the largest absolute bill reductions from wildfire removal and the combined scenario, saving \$500--\$1,300/yr depending on the utility and CARE status. Fixed charge scenarios provide smaller benefits to EV households than to non-adopters because EV households consume more electricity and benefit more from volumetric rate reductions.

\subsection*{Climate zone variation within PG\&E.}

Bill impacts vary across PG\&E's climate zones (Fig.~4). We group the 11 CEC zones into coastal/mild (zones 1--5), inland/moderate (zones 6, 12), and inland/hot (zones 11, 13--14, 16). Inland/hot zones have higher baseline consumption driven by cooling loads, producing larger absolute bill changes under all scenarios.

For non-CARE non-adopters, the 50\% fixed charge increases bills by \$200--\$400/yr in inland/hot zones but produces smaller changes in coastal zones. Wildfire removal saves inland/hot households approximately \$1,200/yr compared to \$700/yr for coastal households. The error bars show substantial within-zone variation, reflecting differences in building size, efficiency, and occupancy.

For PV-plus-storage adopters, non-CARE households in all climate zones see bill increases under 50\% fixed charges, ranging from \$400--\$800/yr. Inland/hot zones show larger increases because the fixed charge represents a larger fraction of their higher baseline bills. Under wildfire removal and the combined scenario, PV households in all zones see bill decreases, with inland/hot zones benefiting more in absolute terms.

\subsection*{Geographic distribution of fixed charge impacts.}

We map the bill impacts of revenue-neutral fixed charge scenarios for non-adopters across all three utility territories (Fig.~5). The left column shows reference TOU bills by climate zone, with CARE bills averaging \$1,500--\$1,800/yr and non-CARE bills averaging \$3,000--\$3,800/yr. The inland Central Valley and desert regions show the highest bills in both income groups, driven by summer cooling loads.

The CPUC fixed charge (center column) produces uniformly blue maps for both CARE and non-CARE households, indicating bill reductions of \$100--\$200/yr statewide. The 50\% fixed charge (right column) reveals the geographic redistributive effect. CARE households see deep blue across the state (savings of \$200--\$500/yr), with the largest reductions in inland areas where baseline consumption and therefore volumetric savings are highest. Non-CARE households show a mixed pattern: coastal and low-consumption areas are light blue (modest savings), while a cluster of climate zones in the San Francisco Bay Area and inland Southern California show light red (bill increases of \$100--\$300/yr). These are areas where non-CARE consumption is high enough that the volumetric rate reduction does not fully offset the fixed charge addition.

The map illustrates a key finding: the geographic incidence of fixed charges tracks climate-driven consumption patterns. Households in hot climates, who consume more electricity for cooling, see larger volumetric rate reductions that offset the fixed charge. Households in mild climates, whose bills are lower to begin with, are more likely to see net bill increases under fixed charge scenarios.

\subsection*{Revenue neutrality and CARE revenue burden.}

We verify that revenue-neutral scenarios collect the same total revenue as the reference rate (Fig.~6a). The benchmark designed rate (F0\_WF0\_ROE0) matches the reference TOU at 100\% for all three utilities, confirming the rate design tool produces internally consistent results. The CPUC fixed charge collects 93--96\% of reference revenue because the approved fixed charge levels (\$6/\$24.15 per month) collect less than the full T\&D share. The 50\% fixed charge collects 97--100\% of reference revenue. Cost-reduction scenarios reduce revenue proportionally: wildfire removal to 73--90\% and the combined scenario to 71--88\%, with PG\&E showing the largest reductions.

CARE customers contribute 12.6--14.9\% of total residential revenue under the reference rate (Fig.~6b). This is lower than their population share (22--30\% of customers) because the CARE discount reduces volumetric rates by 32.5--37\%. Fixed charge scenarios shift the CARE burden further downward: under 50\% fixed charges, the CARE share drops to 11.0\% for SCE and 13.1\% for PG\&E. This occurs because the CARE fixed charge (\$6/month) is 24.8\% of the non-CARE charge (\$24.15/month), a steeper discount than the 63--68\% volumetric rate ratio. Cost-reduction scenarios (wildfire removal and ROE reduction) preserve the CARE share at approximately its reference level because the reductions apply uniformly to volumetric rates.
